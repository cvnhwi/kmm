# Camera Decision System

Use this reference when selecting camera language. It adapts StudioBinder's categories—shot size, framing, angle, movement, lens/focus, and mechanism—into a decision system for AI-video prompting.

## Choose emotional distance with shot size

| Narrative need | Default choice | Effect and caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World, scale, isolation, geography | EWS / establishing | Environment dominates; small facial acting will not read |
| Whole-body action and environment | WS / FS | Best for stunts, locomotion, silhouette, and spatial clarity |
| Action plus readable emotion | MWS / cowboy | Preserves hands, weapons, or body mechanics while bringing the subject closer |
| Dialogue, gesture, balanced intimacy | MS / MCU | Strong neutral baseline; allows later size contrast |
| Reaction, realization, vulnerability | CU | Use at the emotional turn, not continuously by default |
| Critical micro-detail | ECU / insert | Reserve for decisive information; establish context before or after |

Progression has meaning. Moving wide → close increases intimacy, urgency, or realization. Moving close → wide can reveal context, consequence, loneliness, or scale.

## Choose power and psychology with angle

| Intention | Angle | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral, honest observation | Eye level | Default when power framing is unnecessary |
| Power, threat, heroism | Low angle | Keep anatomy and horizon controlled; extreme low angles can distort |
| Vulnerability, surveillance, diminished power | High angle | Avoid using it merely for variety |
| Pattern, choreography, spatial system | Overhead / bird's-eye | Excellent for formations and paths; weak for facial emotion |
| Vast geography | Aerial | Establish scale and routes; distinguish from overhead |
| Unease, imbalance, altered reality | Dutch angle | Use selectively and specify roll direction/degree qualitatively |
| Subjective experience | POV | Define whose view, eye height, gaze target, and body visibility |
| Relationship or dialogue | OTS / two-shot | Preserve eyelines and the 180-degree axis |

## Choose camera motion by narrative verb

| Narrative verb | Movement | Operational description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe, trap, deadpan, let performance lead | Static / locked-off | No camera translation or rotation; subject moves within frame |
| Approach, discover, intensify | Dolly/push-in | Camera translates forward; perspective changes naturally |
| Withdraw, reveal consequence, create distance | Pull-back/dolly-out | Camera translates backward while maintaining or releasing framing |
| Follow locomotion | Tracking/follow | Move beside, ahead of, or behind subject; name relation and offset |
| Reveal laterally, compare spaces | Truck/crab | Horizontal translation; maintain height and aim unless specified |
| Reveal across the frame | Pan | Rotate horizontally from a defined start subject to an end subject |
| Reveal vertical scale | Tilt | Rotate vertically; specify bottom-to-top or top-to-bottom |
| Reframe with energy or hide a cut | Whip pan/tilt | Fast rotation with motivated target and controlled motion blur |
| Encircle power, attraction, conflict | Arc/orbit | Specify arc direction and limited degrees; avoid default full 360° |
| Rise/fall to alter hierarchy or reveal | Pedestal/jib/crane | Translate vertically; distinguish from tilt |
| Human urgency or instability | Handheld | Specify restrained, shoulder-level, or aggressive; avoid random jitter |
| Smooth immersion | Gimbal/Steadicam | Define path and subject relation; “smooth” alone is insufficient |
| Perceptual shock or realization | Dolly zoom | Translation plus opposing focal change; reserve for a true story beat |
| Optical emphasis without perspective shift | Zoom | Focal length changes while camera position stays fixed |
| Transfer attention within depth | Rack focus | Name foreground/background focus targets and the trigger |

## Lens and depth heuristics

- **Wide character (roughly 18–28 mm full-frame):** space, speed, proximity, distortion, strong parallax. Keep faces away from extreme edges.
- **Natural wide/normal (roughly 32–50 mm):** grounded perspective, flexible staging, strong default for moving shots.
- **Portrait/short telephoto (roughly 65–100 mm):** intimacy, compression, cleaner facial rendering, less environmental context.
- **Long lens:** surveillance, compression, graphic layers, difficult tracking and focus.
- **Macro:** micro-detail only; do not imply broad spatial context.
- **Deep focus:** geography and simultaneous foreground/background action.
- **Shallow focus:** isolation and attention control; risky when action crosses depth quickly.

Use ranges as creative guidance, not a claim about exact virtual-camera calibration.

## Movement intensity

- **Stillness:** contemplation, dread, comedy, inevitability.
- **Slow controlled move:** discovery, intimacy, accumulating tension.
- **Moderate tracking:** purposeful action and spatial continuity.
- **Fast acceleration:** pursuit, impact, panic; include a settle or clear terminal frame.
- **Abrupt whip:** transition or sudden attention shift; do not stack with orbit and zoom without a clear reason.

Use acceleration language such as “begins nearly still, accelerates as the subject runs, then decelerates into a stable close-up.” This is more actionable than “fast cinematic camera.”

## Blocking and continuity

- Define where subjects begin, their path, gaze, interaction, and final mark.
- State whether the camera leads, follows, crosses, or remains separate from the action.
- Preserve screen direction across cuts unless a deliberate neutral reset or axis crossing is shown.
- Ensure weapons, hands, faces, and important props remain visible at the moment they matter.
- Let foreground objects motivate reveals and transitions, but avoid occluding identity-critical details.
- In fights, prioritize contact readability and route changes over continuous spinning around the fighters.
